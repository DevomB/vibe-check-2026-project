ONE LINE
Build your opponent. Play it. Get a game review whose grades are proved to follow its stated rules, and never peek at the bot's cards.

WHY THIS WINS (the two judging axes)
- Coolness: a poker table in the browser, a bot you design with one Risk slider, and a chess.com-style review of your play. Anyone gets it.
- Verification: the theorems cover the actual Lean betting steps, shuffle, bot, review and session reachability/rejection. JSON parser/serializer accuracy and browser/Node transport correctness remain tested rather than proved.
- NON-GOAL: a strong bot. The bot does not need to play well. It needs to do exactly what its sliders say, provably.

2. WHAT THE USER SEES

3. THE SLIDERS, IN PLAIN WORDS
RISK (the internal caution number; see the Nov 1 note above), 0 to 100: how heavily the bot weighs a lost chip when deciding whether to call. The player's Risk runs the other way: higher means more risk-seeking.
- Risk 0: calls whenever calling has non-negative average chip gain.
- With no possible ties, a pot-sized bet requires 1/3 win probability at Risk 0, 1/2 at Risk 50, and 3/5 at Risk 100.
- Ties use the chips actually won or lost on a split; a guaranteed profitable split is worth calling at every Risk setting.
- Theorem: for the same decision state and win/tie/loss counts, if the bot calls or raises at a higher Risk, it also continues at every lower Risk. This is a comparison at one decision, not a claim about entire games following different actions.

BLUFF, 0% to 60% (since Nov 1 set by the player's Risk through Sliders.ofRisk, not by its own slider: 0% at Risk 0, 33% at Risk 50, 60% at Risk 100): the target share of designated bluff hands in the bot's board-only river betting set.
- 0% selects no bluff hands. About 33% is a reference from an idealized pot-sized, polarized call/fold game; it does not make this bot a balanced Hold'em strategy. 60% selects more bluff hands.
- It stops at 60% because the bot always bets its strong hands too; past about 70% it would run out of weak hands to bluff with.
- Theorem: the number of bluff hands in that defined betting set matches the X% target, rounded to a whole hand. Actual dealt and played bets can have a different share because of blockers, earlier actions and sampling variation.

Why Bluff is a real percentage: it is the thing we prove, so the number the engine reports (state.sliders.bluffPct) and the number in the theorem are the same number.


4. GAME RULES (exact)
- 2 players. 500 chips each, 1,000 total, no rebuys. Chips are whole numbers.
- One 52-card deck, shuffled every hand by a seeded generator, so any game can be replayed.
- Blinds 5/10 for the whole game. Heads-up rule: the button posts the small blind, acts first preflop and last after the flop.
- Streets: preflop, flop, turn, river, showdown. Standard No-Limit: the minimum bet is the big blind; the minimum raise increment is the last full bet or raise increment; you can always go all-in for less, and an all-in short of a full raise does not reopen the betting or reset that increment.
- Every bet/raise amount is the player's TOTAL contribution on the current street, including chips already committed on that street. UI bounds and JSON use the same units. A call amount is the additional chips paid to call.
- Showdown: best five of seven cards. Split pots split evenly; an odd chip goes to the big blind (the player left of the button).
- The game ends when one player has all 1,000 chips.
- After a hand ends, hold its result and review until the human clicks Next Hand. Distinguish a finished hand from a finished game; do not advance after game-over.


5. THE BOT (exact, for the people coding it)
Inputs: its two cards, the board, the pot, the amount to call, both stacks, the two sliders.

Equity = its chance to win against a random hand, ties counting half:
- Preflop: a fixed table of 169 starting-hand win/tie/loss count records, generated once offline by seeded Monte Carlo (exact preflop equity takes minutes per hand). Equity alone is insufficient for the loss-weight utility. Trusted data, listed in the README with its seed, sample count and generator command.
- Flop: Monte Carlo, 1,000 samples from the seeded generator. An estimate, labeled as one.
- River: exact, by enumerating every possible opponent hand.
- Turn: exact enumeration must include each possible opponent hand AND each possible river card (45,540 outcomes). Benchmark the compiled Lean implementation and choose a fixed exact-or-Monte-Carlo mode before the demo. Record that mode in replay metadata; do not change modes according to per-request machine timing. River remains exact.

Facing a bet (call C chips to win a contestable pot of P, where P includes the matched opponent bet; return any uncallable excess before using P):
- If equity is 75% or more and a raise is legal, raises to the pot-sized target clamped to the legal min/max street totals. If a raise is unavailable, continue to the call/fold utility rule below.
- Otherwise let w, t and l be the win, tie and loss counts from the same enumeration, sample set or preflop table. Calls when:
    100 x (2 x w x P + t x (P - C)) >= 2 x (100 + 2 x Risk) x l x C.
  This uses utility u(g) = g for g >= 0 and u(g) = (1 + Risk/50) * g for g < 0. A call wins P, loses C, or gains (P-C)/2 on a split. At Risk 0 this is ordinary chip EV. Counts from sampling remain estimates.
- Otherwise folds.

Pot-sized raise target: the player's existing street contribution + C + (P + C). For example, a pot of 100 before an opponent bets 50 gives P=150, C=50 and a raise TO 250 if the player had nothing in on that street. Clamp the target to the legal min/max; a short all-in is permitted only when the legal bounds allow it.

No bet to face, preflop to turn:
- If equity is 65% or more and betting/raising is legal, bets pot: target = existing street contribution + P, clamped to the legal min/max. Otherwise checks.

The river, when no bet is being faced (facing a bet still uses the rule above):
- Consider every unordered two-card hand compatible with the board, without using the human's hidden cards. Rank by best-five-of-seven showdown score; break tied scores by the ascending pair of card IDs, with ranks 2..A and suits c,d,h,s.
  Card IDs go rank first, then suit: 2c=0, 2d=1, 2h=2, 2s=3, 3c=4, ..., As=51 (the order of fullDeck in engine/Poker/Spec/Basics.lean). Among hands with tied scores, the hand with the lower IDs ranks higher: compare the lower cards' IDs, then the higher cards' IDs. (Decided Oct 31; the earlier wording did not say which way.)
- For N candidate hands, take the highest-ranked V = floor(30*N/100) hands for value. The fixed card-order tie break is for strategy selection ONLY; tied showdown scores still split the pot.
- For b = bluff_pct, take the lowest-ranked B remaining hands as designated bluffs, where B is V*b/(100-b) rounded to the nearest integer, halves upward. Equivalently, B = floor((2*V*b + (100-b)) / (2*(100-b))). Value and bluff sets are disjoint.
- Its actual hand decides: value hand, bet; bluff hand, bet; anything else, check.
- Opening river bets use the same pot-sized target and legal clamping as other opening bets; if no bet is legal, check. These are strategy labels; on a board where everyone ties, a designated bluff hand does not have lower showdown strength.

All math is in whole numbers. Equities are counts (wins, ties, total), and every inequality is cross-multiplied, so no proof ever touches a decimal.


6. GAME REVIEW (MVP)
- Grades your river call/fold decisions in response to the bot's opening bet from its river betting set. Responses to raises, and raises you make, stay ungraded. "Best" means best among call and fold only.
- The bot's possible hands = every hand its river rule bets with on that board (value plus bluffs), minus any hand blocked by your cards. The MVP does not narrow this by the earlier betting; that is a stretch.
- What calling and folding were each worth is computed exactly with equal weight over those hands, in big blinds. This is exact under the stated board-only range model; ignoring earlier actions makes it an approximation of the bot's actual conditional range.
- Fold is worth 0 from the decision onward; earlier contributions are sunk. Compare ordinary chip EV, not the bot's Risk-weighted utility.
- Label by EV lost compared to the better of call/fold: Best = 0, Good = (0, 0.5) big blinds, Inaccuracy = [0.5, 2), Mistake = [2, 5), Blunder = [5, infinity). Choose the label before rounding for display.
- Every other decision shows "not graded yet".


7. WHAT IS LEAN AND WHAT IS NOT
- Lean 4 (engine/): cards, deck, shuffle, hand evaluator, game state machine, equity, bot, review, and every proof. All pure functions, so we prove theorems directly about the code that runs. No translation tools.
- The local TypeScript game is a rapid prototype and possible test reference. Its tests do not establish correctness of a later Lean implementation. The shipping UI must use the Lean engine.
- Lean libraries: core Lean plus Batteries. No Mathlib in the MVP, so builds stay fast. JSON through Lean's built-in Lean.Json.
- Pin leanprover/lean4:v4.35.0 and matching dependency revisions; commit lake-manifest.json. Only propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound are permitted proof axioms. No native_decide in shipped proofs.
- The Lean program is a command-line binary: one JSON request per line on stdin, one JSON response per line on stdout.
- Web (layout settled in section 15: the Next.js app is under src/, and the TypeScript prototype stays under src/ as a test reference): a small Node server that starts the Lean binary and relays messages, plus the browser UI. NO GAME LOGIC IN JAVASCRIPT.
- Required deployment: one local active game in one browser, started with make install && make run. Public hosting and concurrent games are outside the MVP.
- After new_game, a valid act, or next_hand, Lean runs the bot and automatic street transitions until the next human decision or hand end, then returns one state response. JavaScript does not decide bot actions. Read-only review and rejected commands do not advance play or consume shuffle randomness.
- Tests (tests/): differential tests against our npm library poker-calculations, PINNED TO EXACTLY 4.0.3.
  - Do not use versions below 4.0.0: versions 2.2.0 through 3.2.0 were reported to have an evaluator bug that can skew equity results.
  - The harness, cases and results are in tests/diff/ (run.ts, cases.json, results.json). Its recorded run (Oct 31, Windows, Node 22, seed 20261031) passed 11 of 11 checks against 4.0.3, including hand ranks on 100,000 random deals.
  - Compare hand ranks on 100,000 random deals, exact equity on flop, turn and river spots, 36 preflop starting hands each against a random hand (not hand against hand), and Monte Carlo results within 4 standard errors.
  - Library gotchas: it silently accepts duplicate cards, so the harness deals distinct cards itself; cards go in as "Ah" (capital rank; lowercase "ah" throws); rank hands with evaluateHandStrength; never ask it for exact preflop equity (minutes per call).
- Makefile: "make install" builds the Lean engine and installs the web dependencies; "make run" starts the server and opens the browser. Must work on a fresh clone.


8. THE THEOREMS
The names below are the planned names. Where the Lean names differ, they are given in brackets; engine/comparator.json lists every registered theorem.
Tier 1 (must ship):
- T1 chips_conserved [Lean: step_keeps_chips, startHand_keeps_chips, chips_always_1000]: after every action, your stack + the bot's stack + the pot = 1,000. Chips are never created or destroyed.
- T2 no_duplicate_cards [Lean: shuffle_same_cards, shuffle_fair, no_card_dealt_twice]: the shuffled deck is a rearrangement of the 52 cards, so no card is ever dealt twice.
- T3 legal_only [Lean: accepted_move_is_legal, illegal_move_rejected, player_to_act_may_act, botDecide_legal]: the engine rejects any illegal action and leaves the game unchanged, and the bot only ever picks a legal action.
- T4 risk_monotone: for the same valid decision state, outcome counts and other settings, if the bot continues (calls or raises) at some Risk, it also continues at every lower Risk. This covers our exact loss-weight utility; it is not a statement about how many hands different full-game trajectories play.
- T5 review_sound [Lean: review_worth, review_lost, review_best_iff, review_label_monotone, review_call_best_iff_equity]: for a graded call/fold decision, Best means zero chip EV lost against the better of those two actions under the declared range model, and labels only get worse as the cost grows. It makes no claim about raising.

Tier 2 (should ship):
- T6 enumeration_exact: the exact equity count visits every valid outcome exactly once: opponent hands on the river; opponent-hand/river-card pairs on the turn.
- T7 bluff_frequency: the designated bluff count in the board-only river betting set matches the X% target rounded to a whole hand, using the same set as the actual bot. It does not promise an observed frequency across played games.
- T8 no_hindsight: a review verdict never depends on the bot's actual hidden cards, only on what you could know plus the bot's rules. The review grades decisions, not luck.

Tier 3 (stretch):
- T9 every_hand_ends: every hand finishes in a bounded number of actions.
- T10 evaluator_equals_spec [not built: there is no separate fast evaluator; see SPEC.md]: the fast evaluator gives the same answer as the slow, obviously correct one (best of the 21 five-card hands).

13. JSON MESSAGES (engine <-> Node server), MVP
The browser may omit the seed or send an empty seed string to request a private,
server-generated 64-bit seed. Node supplies that seed to Lean. Public HTTP state replies
set seed to null until gameOver, including between hands, in reviews and in error states.
An entered seed is an intentional replay and may be displayed because the player already
knows it. A finished game's seed is revealed for replay; card and action rules stay in Lean.
Requests, one per line (the HTTP bridge requires entered seeds as strings):
  {"cmd":"new_game","seed":"42","risk":50}                   (Nov 1: bluff_pct was removed; sending it is an error)
  {"cmd":"act","action":"call"}                              (action: fold, check, call, bet, raise)
  {"cmd":"act","action":"raise","amount":120}
  {"cmd":"next_hand"}
  {"cmd":"state"}
  {"cmd":"review","hand":3}
Responses below span lines for readability; each actual response is one JSON line.
These independent examples abbreviate state fields. The complete schemas are in
src/lean/protocol.ts and engine/Engine/Protocol.lean; use those when implementing clients.
State response (players[0] is human, players[1] is bot):
  {"ok":true,"state":{"hand":3,"seed":null,"street":"turn","pot":60,
   "currentBet":60,"minRaise":60,"status":"playing","toAct":0,
   "board":["2c","7d","Js","Qh"],
   "players":[{"cards":["Ah","Kd"],"stack":470,"bet":0},
              {"cards":null,"stack":410,"bet":60,"lastAction":"Bet 60"}],
   "legal":{"fold":true,"check":false,"call":60,"raise":{"min":120,"max":470}},
   "botReasons":null}}
Review response (example: pot 200, call 100, equity 0.24):
  {"ok":true,"state":{ ...current state... },"review":{"hand":3,"decisions":[
   {"street":"river","your_action":"call","label":"Mistake",
    "worth_bb":{"call":-2.8,"fold":0},"equity_had":0.24,"equity_needed":0.333}],
   "bot_reasons":[ ...post-hand reasons... ]}}
Error response:
  {"error":"illegal move","state":{ ...the unchanged state... }}
Cards: rank (2-9, T, J, Q, K, A) + suit (c, d, h, s). Risk travels as a whole number 0-100; the state's sliders field reports it with the two internal numbers it maps to, {"risk":50,"caution":0,"bluffPct":33}. Decimals in responses are for display only; the engine computes exact fractions.
Status: "playing", "handOver", or "gameOver". Only "handOver" accepts next_hand. A finished hand retains its result and review until that command; gameOver ends the match. State pot contains earlier streets only; add both players' current bet values for the displayed pot. Bet/raise amount and min/max are street totals, while call amount is additional chips to pay.
