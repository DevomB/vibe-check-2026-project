2. WHAT THE USER SEES
- Setup screen: two sliders, Risk (0 to 100) and Bluff (0% to 60%), and a Start button.
- Table screen: your two cards, the board, the pot, both stacks, and your legal actions (fold, check, call, bet or raise with an amount). Each bot action shows a one-line reason.
- Review screen: each graded decision with its label (Best, Good, Inaccuracy, Mistake, Blunder), what each option was worth, and the equity you had vs the equity you needed.


3. THE SLIDERS, IN PLAIN WORDS
RISK, 0 to 100: how much better odds the bot needs before it puts chips in.
- Risk 0: calls whenever calling makes money on average. Facing a pot-sized bet, it calls with 33% or better.
- Risk 50: facing the same bet, it needs 50%.
- Risk 100: it needs 60%.
- Theorem: turning Risk up can never make the bot play more hands.

BLUFF, 0% to 60%: the share of the bot's final-round bets that are bluffs.
- 0% never bluffs. 33% is the balanced, game-theory amount for a pot-sized bet. 60% bluffs constantly.
- It stops at 60% because the bot always bets its strong hands too; past about 70% it would run out of weak hands to bluff with.
- Theorem: if the slider says X%, exactly X% of its final-round bets are bluffs (rounded to the nearest hand).

Why not raw 0-to-100 for Bluff too: a real percentage is the thing we prove, so the number on the slider and the number in the theorem are the same number.


4. GAME RULES (exact)
- 2 players. 500 chips each, 1,000 total, no rebuys. Chips are whole numbers.
- One 52-card deck, shuffled every hand by a seeded generator, so any game can be replayed.
- Blinds 5/10 for the whole game. Heads-up rule: the button posts the small blind, acts first preflop and last after the flop.
- Streets: preflop, flop, turn, river, showdown. Standard No-Limit: the minimum bet is the big blind; the minimum raise is the size of the last raise; you can always go all-in for less, and an all-in short of a full raise does not reopen the betting.
- Showdown: best five of seven cards. Split pots split evenly; an odd chip goes to the big blind (the player left of the button).
- The game ends when one player has all 1,000 chips.


5. THE BOT (exact, for the people coding it)
Inputs: its two cards, the board, the pot, the amount to call, both stacks, the two sliders.

Equity = its chance to win against a random hand, ties counting half:
- Preflop: a fixed table of 169 starting-hand equities, generated once offline by seeded Monte Carlo (exact preflop equity takes minutes per hand). Trusted data, listed in the README.
- Flop: Monte Carlo, 1,000 samples from the seeded generator. An estimate, labeled as one.
- Turn and river: exact, by enumerating every possible opponent hand. If the turn is too slow, it falls back to Monte Carlo.

Facing a bet (call C chips to win a pot of P, where P already includes the bet):
- Raises to pot if equity is 75% or more.
- Otherwise calls when: equity x P x 100 >= (100 + 2 x Risk) x (1 - equity) x C. At Risk 0 this is exactly pot odds.
- Otherwise folds.

No bet to face, preflop to turn:
- Bets pot if equity is 65% or more. Otherwise checks.

The river:
- Over every hand the bot could hold on this board, it bets the strongest 30% for value.
- Then it adds the weakest hands as bluffs, exactly enough that bluffs make up Bluff% of its bets (rounded to the nearest hand).
- Its actual hand decides: value hand, bet; bluff hand, bet; anything else, check.

All math is in whole numbers. Equities are counts (wins, ties, total), and every inequality is cross-multiplied, so no proof ever touches a decimal.


6. GAME REVIEW (MVP)
- Grades your river call/fold decisions when the bot bets.
- The bot's possible hands = every hand its river rule bets with on that board (value plus bluffs), minus any hand blocked by your cards. The MVP does not narrow this by the earlier betting; that is a stretch.
- What calling and folding were each worth is computed exactly over those hands, in big blinds.
- Label by how much the decision cost compared to the best option: Best = nothing, Good = under 0.5 big blinds, Inaccuracy = under 2, Mistake = under 5, Blunder = 5 or more.
- Every other decision shows "not graded yet".


7. WHAT IS LEAN AND WHAT IS NOT
- Lean 4 (engine/): cards, deck, shuffle, hand evaluator, game state machine, equity, bot, review, and every proof. All pure functions, so we prove theorems directly about the code that runs. No translation tools.
- Lean libraries: core Lean plus Batteries. No Mathlib in the MVP, so builds stay fast. JSON through Lean's built-in Lean.Json.
- The Lean program is a command-line binary: one JSON request per line on stdin, one JSON response per line on stdout.
- Web (web/): a small Node server that starts the Lean binary and relays messages, plus the browser UI. NO GAME LOGIC IN JAVASCRIPT.
- Tests (tests/): differential tests against our npm library poker-calculations, PINNED TO EXACTLY 4.0.3.
  - NEVER USE ANY VERSION BELOW 4.0.0. Versions 2.2.0 through 3.2.0 have an evaluator bug that made every equity number wrong.
  - 4.0.3 passed 48 of 48 checks on Oct 31, matching an independent full enumeration exactly.
  - Compare hand ranks on 100,000 random deals, exact equity on flop, turn and river spots, a few dozen preflop matchups, and Monte Carlo results within 4 standard errors.
  - Library gotchas: it silently accepts duplicate cards, so the harness deals distinct cards itself; cards go in as "Ah" (capital rank; lowercase "ah" throws); rank hands with evaluateHandStrength; never ask it for exact preflop equity (minutes per call).
- Makefile: "make install" builds the Lean engine and installs the web dependencies; "make run" starts the server and opens the browser. Must work on a fresh clone.


8. THE THEOREMS
Tier 1 (must ship):
- T1 chips_conserved: after every action, your stack + the bot's stack + the pot = 1,000. Chips are never created or destroyed.
- T2 no_duplicate_cards: the shuffled deck is a rearrangement of the 52 cards, so no card is ever dealt twice.
- T3 legal_only: the engine rejects any illegal action and leaves the game unchanged, and the bot only ever picks a legal action.
- T4 risk_monotone: if the bot continues (calls or raises) at some Risk setting, it also continues at every lower setting. Turning Risk up can never make it play more hands. (This is Pratt's comparative risk aversion for our utility; the fully general version is a stretch.)
- T5 review_sound: a decision is labeled Best only if no other legal action was worth more, and labels only get worse as the cost grows.

Tier 2 (should ship):
- T6 enumeration_exact: the exact equity count visits every possible opponent hand exactly once.
- T7 bluff_frequency: if Bluff is set to X%, exactly X% of the bot's river bets are bluffs, rounded to the nearest hand.
- T8 no_hindsight: a review verdict never depends on the bot's actual hidden cards, only on what you could know plus the bot's rules. The review grades decisions, not luck.

Tier 3 (stretch):
- T9 every_hand_ends: every hand finishes in a bounded number of actions.
- T10 evaluator_equals_spec: the fast evaluator gives the same answer as the slow, obviously correct one (best of the 21 five-card hands).

Proof rules (from the organizers' CLAUDE.md):
- Spec files (engine/Poker/Spec/) are written and owned by humans. Claude may write proofs; it never changes a spec or a definition to make a proof easier.
- No sorry, admit, or new axiom on main after Sunday 12 PM. Every main theorem gets a #print axioms check.
- Every main theorem gets a concrete example showing its assumptions can actually hold, and at least one check that a known-bad output is rejected.
- One lake build at a time per checkout.

10. STRETCH LADDER (in this order, after Checkpoint B)
- S1 Proofs screen: every theorem in plain English, with its Lean name.
- S2 The bot's one-line reason is proven to be exactly the condition that produced its action.
- S3 Aggression slider (bet sizes), with a theorem that raising it never makes the bot bet smaller.
- S4 Accuracy % and a luck-vs-skill graph, with a proof that skill + luck = the actual result.
- S5 Pratt's theorem in full generality (needs Mathlib's Jensen inequality), applied to the bot.
- S6 Kuhn poker mode with a proven unbeatable strategy.
- S7 3-player table with side pots, and T1 extended to side pots.
- S8 T10, the evaluator equivalence proof.
- S9 Mystery bot (you read the bot): the sliders are hidden, you play, then you guess its Risk and Bluff, and the review tells you how close you were. Cheap on top of the MVP.
- S10 The bot reads you: it tracks how often you fold and bluffs more only when its own numbers say it pays. Provable: it never bluffs unless its estimate says the bluff is profitable. Not provable: that its read on you is accurate.

13. JSON MESSAGES (engine <-> Node server), MVP
Requests, one per line:
  {"cmd":"new_game","seed":42,"risk":50,"bluff_pct":33}
  {"cmd":"act","action":"call"}                              (action: fold, check, call, bet, raise)
  {"cmd":"act","action":"raise","amount":120}
  {"cmd":"review","hand":3}
State response:
  {"hand":3,"street":"turn","pot":60,"to_call":60,"over":false,
   "board":["2c","7d","Js","Qh"],
   "you":{"cards":["Ah","Kd"],"stack":430},
   "bot":{"stack":510,"last_action":"bet 60","reason":"equity 71%, value bet"},
   "legal":[{"action":"fold"},{"action":"call","amount":60},{"action":"raise","min":120,"max":430}]}
Review response:
  {"hand":3,"decisions":[{"street":"river","your_action":"call","label":"Blunder",
   "worth_bb":{"call":-4.1,"fold":0},"equity_had":0.12,"equity_needed":0.33}]}
Error response:
  {"error":"illegal action","state":{ ...the unchanged state... }}
Cards: rank (2-9, T, J, Q, K, A) + suit (c, d, h, s). Sliders travel as whole numbers (risk 0-100, bluff_pct 0-60). Decimals in responses are for display only; the engine computes exact fractions.


14. THE 5-MINUTE PITCH
0:00 The hook (30 seconds): "When a poker app says the deck is fair, or that you made a mistake, you have to take its word for it. In ours, every one of those claims is checked by a proof."
0:30 Build your opponent (1 minute): set Risk to 100 and Bluff to 0%. Bet big at it: it folds everything but strong hands.
1:30 Change its personality (1 minute): Risk 0, Bluff 60%. Now it fires bluffs on the river.
2:30 The game review (1 minute): make a bad river call, open the review. "Blunder. You needed 33% and had 12% against every hand this bot could have held."
3:30 What is proven (1 minute), in plain words:
  - Chips can never be created or destroyed.
  - No card is ever dealt twice.
  - Every move is legal.
  - Turning the Risk slider up can never make the bot play more hands.
  - The Bluff slider is exact: 33% means 33% of its final bets are bluffs.
  - The review grades your decisions, never your luck.
4:30 The close (30 seconds): what we would build next (reading the other player, the Proofs screen), and thanks.
